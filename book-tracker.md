# Book Progress and Material Ledger

Last updated: 2026-08-06

This is the book-wide editorial dashboard. It tracks chapter progress, the
primary home of major ideas, and the use of anecdotes, repertoire, and invented
examples. Chapter-specific evidence still belongs in each chapter's source
ledger.

The aim is not to prevent recurrence. Recurrence is part of the book's form.
The aim is to distinguish a useful return from an accidental repeat.

## Status vocabulary

### Chapters

- **Not started** — no chapter brief yet.
- **Briefed** — question, promise, scope, examples, and exclusions are set.
- **Researched** — a claim-level source ledger is ready.
- **Drafted** — a complete prose draft exists.
- **Reviewed** — structural, coverage, musical, factual, voice, momentum,
  continuity, and line passes are complete.

### Anecdotes and examples

- **Available** — researched but not committed to a chapter.
- **Reserved** — assigned in an approved chapter brief but not yet used in
  prose.
- **Used—primary** — carries an opening, return, or substantial section.
- **Used—supporting** — appears briefly to sharpen or complicate an argument.
- **Retired** — considered but deliberately excluded; record why.

An anecdote normally has one full telling. A composer is not single-use, and a
work may return if the later passage analyzes genuinely different musical
evidence. Every return should be logged below before it is drafted.

## Incoming material queue

New anecdotes, musical ideas, works, metaphors, objections, and personal notes
from the user enter here before they enter a chapter. Preserve the substance of
the contribution and any supplied source; do not polish away what made it
interesting before deciding what job it can do.

No unresolved incoming material as of 2026-08-06. The Patrick Shiroishi and DJ
Shadow contributions have been researched and moved to the anecdote ledger as
items 31 and 32. Their original wording and all verification caveats are
preserved in `research/06-user-contributed-material.txt`.

For each incoming item:

1. Identify the musical question it illuminates, complicates, or contradicts.
2. Check whether the same story, work, mechanism, or example has already been
   used or reserved.
3. Separate the user's idea from historical claims that require verification.
4. Suggest one primary home and, only when useful, one secondary possibility.
5. Mark it **Held** if it is valuable but does not yet have an earned place.
6. Mark it **Reserved** only when a chapter brief gives it a precise job.
7. Once used, move it into the relevant permanent ledger below and preserve its
   source trail in the chapter-specific source ledger.

An incoming item may reveal that the outline is wrong. In that case, revise the
outline and record the reason rather than forcing the item into the nearest
existing heading.

      nearest drafted chapters.

  example, metaphor, and exercise mechanism.
