29. FOUR OVERSIZED SCORES ASK A QUARTET TO EXPERIENCE MUSICAL SCALE

Musical idea: Extreme duration reorganizes formal memory and makes performers'
bodies part of a work's timescale.

Morton Feldman wrote String Quartet II in 1983 on a commission from the Canadian
Broadcasting Corporation, and the Kronos Quartet gave the premiere. The notated
work was so large that early performances were cut, and Feldman never heard a
complete realization. A member of the FLUX Quartet remembers receiving four
thick, bound, oversized scores about three months before the ensemble's 1999
landmark complete performance at Cooper Union. In the score, a cell may repeat
as many as thirteen times; a section may last fifteen minutes; and recognizable
material may return after an hour. The
musicians cannot depend on the scale of formal memory used for an ordinary
quartet movement.

The duration is also physical. In 1996 Kronos cancelled a planned performance
after rehearsals produced serious back and shoulder problems. FLUX has since
performed the complete work repeatedly, sometimes taking more than six hours.
The point is not that pain certifies artistic seriousness. It is that a duration
of this scale belongs to arms, backs, concentration, and institutional schedules
as surely as it belongs to harmony and motive. A composer who changes musical
scale also changes the conditions under which the music can be carried.

Verification: STRONG for the 1983 commission and premiere, the variable
duration, the cut early performances, FLUX's 1999 complete performance, the
performer's description of the scores and long recurrences, and Kronos's 1996
cancellation. CONTESTED for the frequent claim that FLUX gave the first complete
performance: later performance-history research indicates an uncut Auryn
Quartet performance may have occurred in 1996 and other complete performances
preceded FLUX during 1999. CAUTION on giving one definitive duration: the score
permits a range, tempos differ, and accounts do not always distinguish cut and
complete performances.

30. A NATIONAL BROADCAST CLOCK SPEEDS UP FELDMAN'S QUARTET

Musical idea: An external clock can alter the musical duration it supposedly
only measures.

At the 1983 Toronto premiere of Morton Feldman's `String Quartet II`, the Kronos
Quartet faced a peculiar constraint. David Harrington later recalled that the
CBC broadcast had to finish at exactly midnight, when `O Canada` was scheduled
to begin. The players brought a watch onstage, cast worried glances at it as the
hours passed, and accelerated enough to finish the cut version in just under
four hours.

The anecdote separates clock duration from musical scale with unusual clarity.
The broadcast schedule was not an innocent container around the performance.
Because the players had to obey it, the clock changed their tempo and therefore
the music it was timing.

Verification: STRONG as David Harrington's retrospective first-person account,
quoted in a scholarly thesis that identifies its original publication. The
original 1983 program and IRCAM independently verify the commission, date,
place, and performers. Keep “just under four hours” attached to this particular
cut performance, not to the work's general duration.
