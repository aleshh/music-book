# Source Ledger: Sustain

This ledger separates established events, participant recollections, acoustical
principles, musical analysis, and the chapter's interpretations. It is a
drafting aid, not the book's final citation format.

## Acoustical principles

### Complex tones and spectra

- Claim: Most musical tones contain multiple frequency components. Harmonic
  components occur at whole-number multiples of a fundamental in an idealized
  periodic tone, although actual instrumental spectra and their amplitudes
  change through an event.
- Source: Joe Wolfe, University of New South Wales Music Acoustics, acoustics
  FAQ and sound-spectrum materials.
  https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/musFAQ.html
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Explain as an audible model, not a promise that all musical sounds are
  perfectly periodic or harmonic.

### Beats

- Claim: In the simplest equal-amplitude case, two close frequencies produce
  periodic amplitude fluctuation whose beat rate equals the absolute difference
  between the frequencies. Musicians use this rate in tuning.
- Sources: OpenStax, `University Physics`, section 17.6; UNSW Music Acoustics.
  https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/17-6-beats
  https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/musFAQ.html
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Caveat: Complex instrumental sounds generate many interacting partials, and
  room, balance, vibrato, and position can blur the simple calculation.

### Difference and combination tones

- Claim: Two sustained tones can produce an audible tone related to their
  frequency difference through nonlinear behavior in the ear, a sound source,
  or a reproduction system. This is distinct from slow amplitude beating even
  though both involve frequency differences.
- Source: UNSW Music Acoustics, “Interference beats and Tartini tones” summary
  and FAQ.
  https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/musFAQ.html
- Confidence: HIGH for the phenomenon; CONTEXT-DEPENDENT for audibility.
- Use: Say “may” and specify favorable conditions. Do not promise a phantom
  bass note from every interval.

### Resonance is selective

- Claim: Instrument bodies and rooms support some modes of vibration more
  readily than others. Resonance redistributes and prolongs existing energy; it
  does not create energy from nothing.
- Sources: UNSW Music Acoustics FAQ and instrument-resonance materials; Alvin
  Lucier work documentation below.
  https://www.phys.unsw.edu.au/jw/musFAQ.html
- Confidence: HIGH.

## La Monte Young

### Childhood listening recollections

- Claim: Young has repeatedly recalled wind around the chinks and eaves of his
  childhood Idaho log cabin and later continuous sounds from telephone poles,
  power lines, and transformers as formative listening experiences.
- Source: Peter Dickinson interview with La Monte Young, July 2, 1987, in
  `CageTalk`.
  https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cagetalk/la-monte-young-interview-with-peter-dickinson-new-york-city-july-2-1987/DFEAC6DE5F88A001120CD539F407F376
- Corroboration: MELA Foundation biography and IRCAM biography.
  https://www.melafoundation.org/ly1para8.htm
  https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/composer/la-monte-young/biography
- Confidence: HIGH as consistent autobiographical testimony.
- Caveat: A childhood memory cannot be independently reconstructed. Present it
  as Young's recollection and not as the sole origin of his practice.

### Sustained duration, tuning, and musical encounters

- Claim: Young began sustained-duration work in the 1950s, founded the Theatre
  of Eternal Music in 1962, developed continuous sound environments with
  Marian Zazeela, and studied Kirana vocal music with Pandit Pran Nath for
  decades.
- Source: MELA Foundation biographical information.
  https://www.melafoundation.org/ly1para8.htm
- Confidence: HIGH as official biographical record.
- Cultural use: Name Pandit Pran Nath and the particular Kirana relationship.
  Do not describe Indian classical music as an anonymous source of drones.

## Giacinto Scelsi, `Quattro pezzi su una nota sola`

### Catalogue facts

- Claim: Scelsi composed the four pieces in 1959 for a 25-player chamber
  orchestra; the work lasts approximately fifteen minutes and was first
  performed in Paris in 1960.
- Source: Fondazione Isabella Scelsi, official catalogue.
  https://www.scelsi.it/it/quattro-pezzi-per-orchestra-%28ciascuno-su-una-nota-sola%29/
- Corroboration: IRCAM catalogue confirms the year, publisher, mixed ensemble,
  and approximate duration, though its displayed premiere date conflicts with
  its composition year and should not be used.
  https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/works/work/11685
- Confidence: HIGH for 1959, forces, duration, and publisher; use the
  Foundation for the premiere year.

### A central pitch is not a single frequency

- Claim: Each movement centers on one pitch while microtonal inflections,
  octave placement, dynamics, articulation, density, and orchestration create
  internal motion. The “single note” is therefore a field or center, not a
  literal restriction to one unvarying waveform.
- Sources: Gregory Reish, “Una Nota Sola: Giacinto Scelsi and the Genesis of
  Music on a Single Note,” `Journal of Musicological Research` 25.2 (2006);
  Fondazione Isabella Scelsi journal materials on `Quattro pezzi`.
  https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01411890600613827
  https://www.scelsi.it/dati/pub/39/doc/103.pdf
- Additional analytical source: Bernard Jonathan Black, doctoral thesis,
  City, University of London, discussion and score examples for Scelsi.
  https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/32442/1/Black%20thesis%202024%20PDF-A.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Base exact bar-level claims on a score if later added. The chapter can
  make robust movement-level observations without naming disputed premiere
  details.

### Authorship boundary

- Claim: Scelsi's improvisatory process and the substantial labor of assistants
  in producing scores are historically and ethically important.
- Source bank: `research/04-agency-transmission-and-memory.txt`, anecdote 23.
- Use: Do not tell that story in this chapter. Log the audible work here and
  reserve the authorship question for `Release Control`, where it can receive a
  full, responsible account.

## Alvin Lucier, `I Am Sitting in a Room`

### Work and process

- Claim: First performed in 1969, the work begins with Lucier speaking a text.
  The recording is played into the room, captured again, and repeatedly
  returned. Frequencies shared by the voice and room are reinforced while
  speech intelligibility recedes.
- Sources: Museum of Modern Art collection essay; MIT interview and performance
  documentation; IRCAM survey of Lucier's works.
  https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2015/01/20/collecting-alvin-luciers-i-am-sitting-in-a-room/
  https://mta.mit.edu/video/interview-alvin-lucier
  https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/composer/alvin-lucier/workcourse
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: One sentence is enough to establish the mechanism. Move quickly to
  resonance, cadence, selection, and the changing relation of voice and room.

### Speech and stutter

- Claim: Lucier's text says the process will smooth irregularities in his
  speech. He stuttered, and the work neither conceals that fact nor needs to be
  reduced to autobiography.
- Sources: Lucier score-text as documented by MoMA; IRCAM survey, drawing on
  Lucier's writings and interviews.
  https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2015/01/20/collecting-alvin-luciers-i-am-sitting-in-a-room/
  https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/composer/alvin-lucier/workcourse
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Caveat: Do not call the transformation a cure, erase Lucier from the ending,
  or describe the room as an autonomous composer. He chose the text, room,
  pacing, level, and stopping point.

## Chapter-specific interpretations

The following are the book's analytical proposals rather than statements made
by the cited artists:

- Sustaining is the maintenance of a changing relation among energy, spectrum,
  tuning, body, room, and attention.
- A drone, pedal, and reference tone are jobs, not fixed instrument types or
  registers.
- A release belongs to the sustained event because it reveals which energy the
  performer supplied and which continuation belonged to instrument or room.
- Stable pitch can make internal motion more audible, but motion requires a
  reference; changing every dimension at once may erase the very interior a
  composer hopes to reveal.
