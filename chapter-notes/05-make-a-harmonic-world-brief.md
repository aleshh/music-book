# Section Brief: Make a Harmonic World

## Central question

How can pitches acquire attraction, distance, friction, and return without
requiring a conventional chord progression or cadence?

## Section promise

By the end of the section, the reader should be able to distinguish a pitch
collection from a harmonic world; establish gravity through emphasis, bass,
register, recurrence, voicing, and voice-leading; use modes and fields without
making every note functionally equal; keep a stable sonority internally active;
and preserve ambiguity without allowing harmony to become interchangeable
atmosphere.

## Central claim

A harmonic world is not merely a list of permitted pitches. It is a patterned
distribution of weight among them. Functional progression is one powerful way
to create weight, but not the only one. A center can be established through
duration, bass position, registral return, melodic orbit, recurring intervals,
or the behavior of individual voices. Harmony can therefore have gravity
without treating every departure as a problem that cadence must solve.

The four locations of change organize the section:

1. Material: the collection may remain fixed or nearly fixed.
2. Relationships: voicing, bass, interval, and voice-leading alter function.
3. Listener: repetition and memory make some pitches feel central or remote.
4. Context: tuning, instrument, register, and resonance change an interval's
   physical and expressive behavior.

## Opening and return

Open with Debussy hearing Javanese musicians at the 1889 Paris Exposition, then
complicate the familiar conversion story. The encounter challenged inherited
European categories without becoming a simple recipe or innocent transfer of
effects.

Return to the encounter at the end: a useful harmonic meeting does not supply a
palette to copy; it makes one's existing categories newly questionable. From
there, turn toward `Drift`: once several lines share a harmonic climate, must
they also share one clock?

## Primary encounters

### Claude Debussy, the Paris Exposition and `Voiles`

- Use the exposition as a historically situated challenge to tonic-dominant
  inevitability, not as a one-scene origin myth for Debussy's later music.
- Analyze `Voiles` as a field whose outer spans largely use one whole-tone
  collection while a persistent low B-flat, register, recurrence, gesture, and
  a brief pentatonic region create hierarchy and form.
- A nominally symmetrical collection can acquire local gravity through
  compositional behavior.

### Lou Harrison, William Colvig, and Old Granddad

- The instruments, their just tuning, their resonators, and the music written
  for them were developed together.
- An interval is not only a staff distance. Tuning and material determine its
  beating, fusion, decay, and weight.
- Preserve Colvig's essential labor and distinguish the American gamelan from
  Indonesian ensembles and traditions.

## Supporting encounter

### Arvo Pärt, `Spiegel im Spiegel`

- This is a deliberate return to Pärt through a different work and question.
- An F-major triadic field can remain while a solo line repeatedly leaves and
  returns to A, creating melodic home inside a larger harmonic home.
- Stepwise voice-leading, phrase expansion, register, and alternating triadic
  placement create motion without progression.

## Required theoretical ground

- Pitch collection versus field, mode, center, and route.
- Harmonic gravity created by bass, duration, recurrence, register, melodic
  emphasis, interval inventory, and phrase placement.
- The difference between remaining in a key and repeatedly performing its
  functional syntax.
- Modes as environments whose scale degrees acquire character through use.
- Pedal and reference tone as harmonic agents once other pitches change their
  relation to them; do not repeat Sustain's acoustic taxonomy.
- Voicing and register as structural choices rather than cosmetic spacing.
- Voice-leading as motion inside an apparently unchanged collection or chord.
- Common tones, contrary motion, parsimonious motion, and registral transfer.
- Harmonic ambiguity as the coexistence of supported hearings, not mere lack of
  information.
- Tuning as a property of intervals and ensembles; enough just-intonation
  explanation to make Harrison audible without becoming a tuning manual.

## Boundaries

- `Sustain` owns spectrum, beating, resonance, and the acoustic interior of a
  tone. This section invokes those only when they alter harmonic relation.
- `Limit` owns collection restriction and Pärt's `Für Alina` anecdote. This
  section asks how pitches inside a field acquire unequal weight and uses a
  different Pärt work.
- `Repeat` owns recurrence as a general formal process. Recurrence appears here
  only as one source of harmonic gravity.
- `Drift` owns independent clocks. Polyphonic examples here change harmonic
  relation without developing asynchronous time.
- `Color` owns timbre in full. Harrison appears because tuning, instrument, and
  harmony were designed together, not as a second general timbre argument.

## Failure modes to address

- Selecting a scale and assuming it has become an environment.
- Treating every scale degree as equal and calling the result nonhierarchical.
- Using open fifths and widely spaced triads as automatic atmosphere.
- Moving every voice enough to make a static field sound busy.
- Preserving ambiguity by withholding all evidence.
- Using a drone to disguise unrelated chords rather than change their meaning.
- Treating just intonation as purity or equal temperament as corruption.
- Turning another musical tradition into an anonymous supply of scales and
  colors.

## Exercises

### Notice: Give One Pitch Weight

Use a five-note collection and establish three different centers without
changing the inventory. Change only recurrence, duration, bass, and register.

### Isolate: One Collection, Three Voicings

Hold one pitch collection through a short form. Create departure and return by
revoicing and registral transfer, with no new pitch and no conventional cadence.

### Complicate: Two Plausible Homes

Build a field that supports two credible centers. Let performers or sections
alter which hearing is stronger while preserving evidence for the other.

## Working length

Approximately 5,300–6,500 words.

## Desired ending

Return to Debussy's encounter as a lesson in revising categories rather than
collecting surfaces. End with several lines recognizing the same harmonic
weather while beginning to move by different measures of time.
