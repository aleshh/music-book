# Source Ledger: Experience Time — Making Duration Perceptible

This ledger separates established events, participant recollections, musical
analysis, and the chapter's own synthesis. It is a drafting aid, not the final
citation format.

## Morton Feldman, `String Quartet II`

### Work, commission, premiere, and notated scale

- Claim: Feldman composed `String Quartet II` in 1983 on commission from the
  Canadian Broadcasting Corporation. The Kronos Quartet gave its premiere in
  Toronto on December 4, 1983.
- Source: IRCAM work record.
  https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/work/string-quartet-ii
- Source: Original New Music Concerts program.
  https://www.newmusicconcerts.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/December-4-1983-Concert-Program-1.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Caution: Early Kronos performances used cuts. “Premiere” and “first complete
  performance” must not be conflated.

### Duration and score

- Claim: The score specifies a variable span; IRCAM gives three and a half to
  five and a half hours. Universal Edition's current score has 130 pages.
- Source: IRCAM work record; Universal Edition score page.
  https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/work/string-quartet-ii
  https://www.universaledition.com/en/String-Quartet-Nr.-2/P0001744
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Avoid declaring one canonical runtime. Performances vary, and the FLUX
  recording/performance can exceed six hours.

### FLUX Quartet's complete performance and player account

- Claim: FLUX Quartet gave a landmark complete performance in 1999 at Cooper
  Union. A member recalls receiving four thick, bound, oversized scores about
  three months beforehand.
- Source: FLUX Quartet first-person page.
  https://www.fluxquartet.com/feldman2.html
- Corroboration: Mode Records, `Feldman Edition 6`.
  https://moderecords.com/catalog/112feldmancd/
- Confidence: HIGH for the complete FLUX performance and the player's account;
  CONTESTED for “first complete.” Later performance-history research indicates
  that the Auryn Quartet may have played the work uncut in February 1996 and
  that four European quartets did so before FLUX in 1999. Do not call FLUX's
  performance the first without this qualification.
- Complicating source: Nicholas W. Miskey, `Elusive Quartet, Imaginary Songs`,
  University of Victoria thesis, pp. 5–6 and note 14.
  https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/71a4f3f9-fb4c-43f2-86e7-570b873cd248/content
- Claim: Cells may repeat as many as thirteen times; some sections last fifteen
  minutes or more; recapped material may return after sixty minutes or more.
- Source: FLUX Quartet first-person page.
- Confidence: HIGH as a performer's score-informed account.

### Performer endurance and the 1996 cancellation

- Claim: Kronos cancelled a scheduled 1996 performance after rehearsals caused
  significant back and shoulder problems; the work requires continuous playing
  without a normal break.
- Source: contemporary report derived from the `San Francisco Chronicle`,
  reprinted by `Deseret News`, August 25, 1996.
  https://www.deseret.com/1996/8/25/19262220/kronos-quartet-cancels-feldman-date/
- Corroboration: Arts at MIT account of FLUX performances.
  https://arts.mit.edu/flux-quartet-tackles-morton-feldmans-epic-avant-garde-string-quartet-ii/
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Discuss bodily limits without sensationalizing pain or implying that
  injury proves the work's importance.

### Form and scale

- Claim: In a 1985 interview, Feldman distinguished thinking about form up to
  roughly an hour from thinking about scale after roughly ninety minutes.
- Source: Metropolitan Museum of Art essay citing John Rockwell's 1985 Feldman
  interview.
  https://www.metmuseum.org/perspectives/calder-quartet-morton-feldman
- Corroboration: Nonesuch liner notes for `Piano and String Quartet`.
  https://ia802206.us.archive.org/15/items/cd_morton-feldman-piano-and-string-quartet_kronos-quartet-with-aki-takahashi/cd_morton-feldman-piano-and-string-quartet_kronos-quartet-with-aki-takahashi.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Quote only the brief contrast between form and scale, then explain it.
  Do not treat ninety minutes as a law.

### The broadcast deadline at the 1983 premiere

- Claim: David Harrington later recalled that the CBC broadcast had to end at
  midnight, when `O Canada` was scheduled. Kronos brought a watch onstage,
  checked it anxiously, and accelerated to finish just under four hours.
- Source: Harrington, “Morton Feldman: String Quartet II,” quoted and sourced in
  Nicholas W. Miskey, `Elusive Quartet, Imaginary Songs`, pp. 7–8.
  https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/71a4f3f9-fb4c-43f2-86e7-570b873cd248/content
- Confidence: HIGH as Harrington's retrospective first-person account.
- Use: Shows an institutional clock altering the musical duration it measures.

## Morton Feldman, `Triadic Memories`

### Disorienting memory

- Claim: In his essay “Crippled Symmetry,” Feldman describes a section in which
  chords are repeated in unequal counts. After forgetting the preceding chord,
  he reconstructed the sequence, rearranged it, and changed the counts as a
  deliberate formalization of disoriented memory.
- Primary source: Morton Feldman, “Crippled Symmetry,” `RES: Anthropology and
  Aesthetics` 2 (1981/82).
  https://doi.org/10.1086/RESv2n1ms20166669
- Accessible reproduction and performer commentary: Jean-Luc Fafchamps,
  `Triadic Memories` booklet note.
  https://jeanlucfafchamps.eu/en/triadic-memories-by-morton-feldman/
- Scholarly analysis: Clark Lunberry, “Remembrance of Things Present.”
  https://www.cnvill.net/files/mflunberry.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Paraphrase. The chapter may quote “formalizing a disorientation of
  memory,” but should not duplicate Chapter 6's general account of repetition.

## Éliane Radigue, `Trilogie de la Mort`

### Dates, parts, and duration

- Claim: `Trilogie de la Mort` was created between 1985 and 1993 and comprises
  `Kyema`, `Kailasha`, and `Koumé`; the Centre Pompidou catalogue gives a total
  duration of 168 minutes and 47 seconds.
- Source: Centre Pompidou collection record.
  https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/cL9jRpp
- Confidence: HIGH.

### Slowly changing internal proportions

- Claim: Radigue said the drone was not an end in itself but a means of slowly
  changing the proportions of a sound's constituents, with the path between
  states almost unnoticeable.
- Source: Éliane Radigue, Julia Eckhardt, and Rhodri Davies, “Listening to
  Éliane,” `Contemporary Music Review` 43 (2024), transcript.
  https://researchonline.rca.ac.uk/5869/1/Listening%20to%20%20liane.pdf
- Corroboration: IRCAM's work survey describes fundamentals, harmonics,
  micro-beats, pulsations, and patient listening.
  https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/composer/eliane-radigue/workcourse
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Describe audible consequence, not equipment. Reserve detailed acoustics
  and the later instrumental collaboration process for `Sustain` and
  `Release Control`.

## Olivier Messiaen, `Abîme des oiseaux`

### Work and movement

- Claim: `Quatuor pour la fin du Temps` dates from 1940, is scored for violin,
  clarinet, cello, and piano, and comprises eight movements; `Abîme des oiseaux`
  is the third, for solo clarinet.
- Source: Durand-Salabert-Eschig Messiaen catalogue, pp. 9–10.
  https://www.durand-salabert-eschig.com/-/media/Images/DSE/PDFs/Brochures/M/messiaen_olivier.ashx?hash=3E63E3E1ECC941DF3E7484C93E7BBAC801DE7D88&la=en-GB
- Confidence: HIGH.

### Two temporal characters

- Claim: Messiaen's score preface identifies the abyss with time, sorrow, and
  weariness, and birds with desire for light. The movement places extremely
  slow, long-breathed clarinet material against quick birdsong figures.
- Source: Anthony Pople, `Messiaen: Quatuor pour la fin du Temps`, chapter 3,
  Cambridge University Press.
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/71CEFF1F9D5E14F707D20E34C5F1C77E/9781139166928c3_p40-46_CBO.pdf/abime-des-oiseaux.pdf
- Source: published score and recordings; performance description requires a
  listening check before final publication.
- Confidence: HIGH for program and musical contrast.
- Caution: Attribute the theological/programmatic meaning to Messiaen. Do not
  use the prison-camp premiere anecdote in this chapter.

## Experienced time and analytical cautions

### Factors influencing musical time

- Claim: Grouping, event density, attention, temporal patterning, and bodily or
  emotional response can all influence experienced musical time.
- Source: Kristina Knowles, `Experiencing Musical Time`, Oxford University
  Press, 2025, chapter 3.
  https://academic.oup.com/book/60846/chapter/529821729
- Source: Knowles and Richard Ashley, “Measuring Experienced Time While
  Listening to Music,” in `Performing Time`, Oxford University Press, 2023.
  https://academic.oup.com/book/56186/chapter/443058483
- Confidence: HIGH as a survey of current research.
- Use: Present these as interacting variables, not deterministic recipes. Do
  not claim that sparse music always feels longer or dense music always feels
  shorter.

### Felt passage and remembered span are not identical

- Claim: The apparent speed of time while an event unfolds and the duration
  attributed to it in memory can diverge; event density and boundaries can
  affect them differently.
- Source: Knowles, chapter 3, above.
- Corroboration outside music: Roseboom et al., “Distinctive features of
  experiential time,” `Consciousness and Cognition` 118 (2024).
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2024.103635
- Confidence: MODERATE for direct transfer to a composer's practice. The
  corroborating experiment used short videos, not extended music.
- Use: State as a useful distinction and listening question, not a scientific
  guarantee.

### Induced and represented timelessness

- Claim: Music may represent or suggest suspended time without literally
  causing every listener to lose awareness of clock time.
- Source: Jason Noble, “What Can the Temporal Structure of Auditory Perception
  Tell Us about Musical ‘Timelessness’?”, `Music Theory Online` 24.3 (2018).
  https://www.mtosmt.org/ojs/index.php/mto/article/view/271
- Confidence: HIGH as a theoretical distinction.
- Use: This distinction helps the chapter avoid vague claims that a piece
  “stops time.”

## Author's theoretical synthesis

### The clocks music gives us

- Status: AUTHOR'S FRAMEWORK, informed by the sources above.
- Claim: Pulse, meter, breath, decay, phrase, harmonic rhythm, recurrence, and
  bodily effort can each function as an audible clock. A work can align,
  separate, obscure, or remove them.
- Caution: `Drift` owns the full account of multiple ensemble clocks.

### Event rate, event magnitude, grouping, and continuity

- Status: AUTHOR'S MUSICAL ANALYSIS.
- Claim: Duration is shaped not only by how often events happen but by how much
  difference each event introduces, how events are grouped, and whether
  boundaries are articulated or elided.
- Caution: Offer these as compositional variables and experiments, not as a
  formula for controlling subjective time.
