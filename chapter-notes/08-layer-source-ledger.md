# Source Ledger: Layer — Turning Relationship into Form

This ledger separates established events, participant recollections, musical
analysis, and the chapter's interpretations. It is a drafting aid, not the
book's final citation format.

## Perceptual grouping, streams, and layers

### Parts on the page and streams in the ear are not identical

- Claim: Events close in time and similar in pitch, loudness, timbre, and
  spatial position tend to group into an auditory stream; abrupt differences
  can segregate streams. A written part can divide perceptually, and several
  sources can fuse.
- Source: Stephen McAdams, Meghan Goodchild, and Kit Soden, “A Taxonomy of
  Orchestral Grouping Effects,” `Music Theory Online` 28.3 (2022), especially
  sections 4–5.
  https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.22.28.3/mto.22.28.3.mcadams.php
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Explain in plain musical terms. Do not imply hard thresholds that operate
  identically for every listener or performance.

### Blend and segregation are matters of degree

- Claim: Timing, tuning, balance, attack, spectral character, and brightness
  affect whether concurrent instruments blend, partly blend, or remain
  separately audible.
- Source: McAdams, Goodchild, and Soden, sections 4.13–5.2.
  https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.22.28.3/mto.22.28.3.mcadams.php
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Support the continuum from line to mass; avoid a technical acoustics
  detour.

## Patrick Shiroishi

### Solo European tour

- Claim: Shiroishi's official archive labels his November 3–14, 2024 dates a
  “Solo European Tour,” ending at Cafe OTO in London.
- Source: Patrick Shiroishi, official show archive.
  https://www.patrickshiroishi.com/shows.html
- Confidence: HIGH.

### Two solo approaches and live self-layering

- Claim: Shiroishi distinguishes an acoustic solo approach from one using
  saxophone through guitar pedals. He says the latter began almost a decade
  earlier with distortion and a loop pedal, and describes loops, a frozen
  drone, overtones, and distortion as ways of sustaining a solo span and
  serving what he needs to say.
- Source: Charlie Looker, “Patrick Shiroishi's Sonic Combat Against Erasing the
  Past,” `The Tonearm`, September 18, 2025, especially interview lines 128–38.
  https://www.thetonearm.com/patrick-shiroishi-saxophone-sonic-resistance-to-erasing-the-past/
- Confidence: HIGH as first-person account.
- Caveat: Do not imply that every solo performance is electronic or loop based.
  Omit operational details that do not change the musical argument.

### Cafe OTO performance sequence

- Claim: In the November 14, 2024 recording, unadorned saxophone tones lead to
  looped voice-and-instrument refrains, saxophone lines in their gaps, a swell
  of bass tones, circular breathing, melodic attacks, recorded speech, a
  clearing, and renewed layering over about thirty-five minutes.
- Source: Cafe OTO, Patrick Shiroishi artist and release page.
  https://www.cafeoto.co.uk/artists/patrick-shiroishi/
- Confidence: HIGH for the venue's description of its own recording.
- Caveat: This is editorial listening prose, not a score or Shiroishi's stated
  formal plan. Present the audible sequence without borrowing its metaphors.

### Layered historical voices in `Forgetting Is Violent`

- Claim: Shiroishi describes beginning the record with the recorded voice of a
  Hiroshima survivor layered over itself; he associates the layering with
  anxiety and identifies the album's subject as historical memory, racism, war,
  and his family's incarceration.
- Source: Looker, `The Tonearm`, lines 151–62.
  https://www.thetonearm.com/patrick-shiroishi-saxophone-sonic-resistance-to-erasing-the-past/
- Confidence: HIGH as first-person account.
- Caveat: Preserve the identity and historical subject of the voice. Do not use
  it as anonymous atmospheric material or imply that the survivor is a member
  of Shiroishi's family.

## György Ligeti, `Atmosphères`

### Work, premiere, and immediate repeat

- Claim: `Atmosphères` was composed in 1961 and premiered at Donaueschingen on
  October 22 with Hans Rosbaud conducting the Southwest German Radio Orchestra;
  the audience demanded an immediate repeat.
- Sources: Universal Edition work history; IRCAM work record; official Ligeti
  work page.
  https://www.universaledition.com/en/Works/Atmospheres/P0006870
  https://resources.ircam.fr/en/work/atmospheres
  https://en.gyorgy-ligeti.com/work/atmosph%C3%A8res
- Confidence: HIGH.

### Micropolyphony and formal means

- Claim: All eighty-nine instrumentalists have individual parts. Densely
  superposed and interwoven lines cause individual identities to dissolve;
  Ligeti called the method micropolyphony and later considered “saturated
  polyphony” more accurate. Density, dynamics, register, and transformations of
  tone color articulate the form.
- Sources: IRCAM work commentary; official Ligeti work page and composer's note.
  https://resources.ircam.fr/en/work/atmospheres
  https://en.gyorgy-ligeti.com/work/atmosph%C3%A8res
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Caveat: Do not say the work literally has no motion. Ligeti's statement that
  it contains no rhythmic or melodic motif concerns foreground identity, not an
  absence of notated rhythm or line.

### Central registral rupture

- Claim: Near the middle, extreme high sounds clear abruptly and reveal busy
  activity in the extreme low register, providing a sharp formal articulation
  within otherwise gradual transformations.
- Source: IRCAM work commentary.
  https://resources.ircam.fr/en/work/atmospheres
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Analyze clearing as a relational event, not merely a dramatic loudness
  change.

## Phill Niblock

### Superimposition and thick masses

- Claim: Niblock describes making structures from many simultaneous long tones,
  including close tunings whose superimposition produces additional tones and
  thick sound masses.
- Source: Phill Niblock interview by Marcelo Aguirre and Daniel Varela, Ars
  Electronica Archive, pp. 1–2.
  https://archive.aec.at/media/assets/e2a23af417a2344fe3a23e652924091f.pdf
- Corroboration: Notes for `Working Touch`, describing one or two recorded
  instruments expanded into environments of as many as thirty-two tracks with
  added microtonal tones.
  https://phillniblock.bandcamp.com/album/working-touch
- Confidence: HIGH.

### `Early Winter`

- Claim: Niblock says `Early Winter` uses about fifty-five tracks over roughly
  forty-five minutes. He characterizes it as seeming similar moment to moment
  while sounding markedly different when sampled at five-minute intervals.
- Source: Niblock interview, p. 2.
  https://archive.aec.at/media/assets/e2a23af417a2344fe3a23e652924091f.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH as first-person description.
- Caveat: The figures are approximate in the interview. Attribute the claim to
  Niblock rather than converting it into definitive catalogue metadata.

## Chapter-specific interpretations

The following are the book's analytical proposals rather than statements made
by the cited artists:

- A captured layer has memory without reciprocal listening.
- A layer is a current perceptual role, not simply a stored or notated part.
- Density has several separable dimensions and cannot be inferred by counting
  sources alone.
- Clearing interprets the preceding accumulation by revealing what can still be
  heard or remembered after it.
