# Source Ledger: Make a Harmonic World

This ledger separates established history, score evidence, acoustical facts,
and the section's interpretations.

## Claude Debussy, the 1889 Exposition, and `Voiles`

### The encounter and its historical frame

- Claim: Debussy heard Javanese performers at the 1889 Paris Exposition and
  later wrote admiringly of Javanese musical nuance while criticizing the
  narrowness of tonic-dominant habits.
- Sources: Oxford University Press study of Debussy and the exposition;
  Ralph P. Locke's scholarly study of Debussy, gamelan, and colonial display,
  which reproduces and contextualizes the January 22, 1895 letter to Pierre
  Louÿs.
  https://academic.oup.com/book/56282/chapter-abstract/445227930
  https://escholarship.org/content/qt2fp1g1cw/qt2fp1g1cw_noSplash_2c0c7cb0c030080b456f9429ba4da999.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Caveat: The exposition was a colonial presentation, not transparent access
  to an entire tradition. Do not claim that one encounter caused a stylistic
  conversion or that a later Debussy passage directly copies gamelan without
  specific evidence.

### `Voiles`

- Claim: The outer spans of the second prelude in Book I draw almost entirely
  on one whole-tone collection, with a short pentatonic central passage and
  localized chromatic exceptions. A recurring low B-flat, registral placement,
  characteristic gestures, and phrase return give a nominally even collection
  local weight and form.
- Primary source: Debussy's 1910 Durand score.
  https://s9.imslp.org/files/imglnks/usimg/2/24/IMSLP406068-PMLP02394-No.2_Voiles.pdf
- Analytical sources: David J. Code, “Parting the veils of Debussy's
  `Voiles`,” `Scottish Music Review` 1.1; University of KwaZulu-Natal study of
  tonality in Book I; Bristol thesis discussion of the low B-flat pedal and
  whole-tone hierarchy.
  https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/7496/
  https://researchspace.ukzn.ac.za/bitstreams/dee4389f-0725-44fe-87aa-78ef5a154af3/download
  https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/34496856/386226.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH for collection, pedal, and form; INTERPRETIVE for how the
  local gestures distribute harmonic weight.

## Arvo Pärt, `Spiegel im Spiegel`

- Claim: Written in 1978 and originally commissioned for violin and piano, the
  work places a stepwise solo melody beside a three-note piano accompaniment.
  Ascending and descending phrases expand one note at a time and return to the
  melodic center A; tintinnabuli notes follow a fixed relation above and below.
- Source: Arvo Pärt Centre official work page.
  https://www.arvopart.ee/en/arvo-part/work/545/
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Additional analytical claim: The work inhabits an F-major triadic and
  diatonic field while giving the third, A, a distinct melodic centrality.
- Corroborating source: Andrew Shenton, analysis of `Spiegel im Spiegel`.
  https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/5/2/361
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Boundary: Do not retell the `Für Alina` search, dedication, or deliberate
  exceptions used in Section 3.

## Lou Harrison, William Colvig, and Old Granddad

### Construction and collaboration

- Claim: Harrison and Colvig constructed Old Granddad in 1971 from aluminum
  slabs, conduit, oxygen tanks, cans, resonators, and other materials. Colvig's
  craft and Harrison's tuning design were both essential.
- Sources: Other Minds archival programme; Harrison first-person American
  Mavericks interview; Rice University dissertation on the instruments.
  https://www.otherminds.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/om22program_lorez.pdf
  https://musicmavericks.publicradio.org/features/interview_harrison.html
  https://repository.rice.edu/items/258b58a7-bc6c-4374-b9d8-42cafb43aa20
- Confidence: HIGH.

### Tuning and cultural context

- Claim: Old Granddad uses a just diatonic tuning approximating a D-major
  collection, with intervals built from simple whole-number relations rather
  than twelve equal divisions of the octave. It was deliberately called an
  American gamelan and preceded Harrison's deeper practical study with Javanese
  teachers and later Javanese-style instruments.
- Sources: Other Minds archival programme; Harrison interview; Rice
  dissertation.
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Caveat: Do not equate the instruments with a Javanese gamelan, erase Colvig,
  or present Indonesian practice as a collection of resonant objects.

## Section-specific interpretations

- A collection becomes a harmonic world when its pitches acquire unequal and
  memorable weights through behavior.
- Harmonic gravity can be distributed among bass, register, duration,
  recurrence, melodic center, tuning, and voice-leading rather than delegated
  entirely to chord progression.
- Ambiguity is the coexistence of supported hearings. An absence of evidence is
  not automatically productive ambiguity.
