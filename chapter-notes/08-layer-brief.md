# Chapter Brief: Layer — Turning Relationship into Form

## Central question

How can a piece develop by changing what the listener can separate, combine,
follow, or recover within a field of simultaneous sound?

## Chapter promise

By the end of the chapter, the reader should understand a layer as a perceptual
and formal role rather than merely another part or recorded track. The reader
should be able to distinguish number of parts from audible density; shape
foreground, masking, fusion, saturation, clearing, and residue; and design a
form in which relations among substantially stable strands carry the change.

## Central claim

A layer is not defined by being present. It is defined by being heard as a
coherent activity in relation to something else. Layering becomes formal when
those relations change: a line becomes texture, several strands fuse into one
mass, a background masks a former subject, or subtraction reveals an identity
that accumulation had concealed.

The chapter is organized primarily by change in relationships, with listener
and material as secondary locations:

1. Relationships: strands separate, fuse, mask, reinforce, oppose, and trade
   foreground roles.
2. Listener: acclimation and directed attention change which internal activity
   can be followed.
3. Material: entrances, departures, register, activity, and articulation alter
   the field when a relational threshold is needed.

## Opening and return

Open with Patrick Shiroishi's November 2024 solo performance at Cafe OTO. A
plain saxophone line is captured and begins repeating; Shiroishi's present line
must now live beside his recent past. Follow the performance through
accumulation, a clearing, and renewed intensity.

Return to the same asymmetry at the end. A captured layer can preserve an
action but cannot listen back. The performer makes form by continually deciding
what the earlier self now is: partner, ground, obstruction, memory, or material
to be cleared.

## Primary encounters

### Patrick Shiroishi, 2024 Cafe OTO solo performance and solo practice

- Live self-layering as an audible relation between present and captured action.
- Phrase becomes refrain, refrain becomes environment, and new lines occupy or
  disturb its gaps.
- The loop is not a neutral multiplier: it has fixed memory and unequal agency.
- Shiroishi's use of layered historical voices in `Forgetting Is Violent`
  prevents looping from being treated as an automatic sign of calm.

### György Ligeti, `Atmosphères`

- Many exact individual parts become perceptually unavailable as individual
  melodies.
- Micropolyphony, or Ligeti's later phrase “saturated polyphony,” as a way of
  composing the behavior of a sound mass through precise internal lines.
- Density, register, dynamics, and sudden clearing carry large-scale form.
- The 1961 audience's request for an immediate repeat shows that a textural
  premise can be legible even when melody and pulse are withheld.

## Supporting encounter

### Phill Niblock, `Early Winter` and long-tone practice

- Recorded instrumental tones layered in close tunings produce emergent tones,
  beating, and a thick field.
- A mass can seem locally stable while differing greatly across longer spans.
- Contrast with Ligeti: Niblock often minimizes attack, phrase, and individual
  voice identity; the interaction among sustained strata supplies the event.

## Required theoretical ground

- Layer, voice, stream, stratum, texture, and mass as related but nonidentical
  ways of hearing simultaneous material.
- Perceptual grouping through continuity, register, timbre, articulation,
  rhythm, spatial position, and common motion.
- A notated part may divide into two audible streams; several parts may fuse
  into one audible object.
- Foreground as a changing job, briefly recalled from `Attend` but not retaught.
- Line becoming texture and texture becoming line.
- Density as several independent choices: number of sources, event rate,
  registral occupation, spectral congestion, dynamic pressure, and similarity.
- Masking and revelation without relying only on volume or muting.
- Register as a map of proximity, distance, weight, and available space.
- Accumulation, saturation, threshold, clearing, and residue as formal verbs.
- Layer profiles: stable stack, rotating hierarchy, gathering, erosion,
  alternation, sudden exposure, and recurring saturation.

## Boundaries

- `Attend` owns the general theory of focal, peripheral, and distributed
  listening. This chapter uses attention only to explain the perception of
  simultaneous strands.
- `Experience Time` owns acclimation and duration in general.
- `Repeat` owns recurrence, invariants, and accumulation as repetition.
- `Drift` owns phase, asynchronous clocks, and coordination without one grid.
- `Color` owns the internal identity and transformation of a tone; Layer owns
  relations among strands, even when timbre helps keep them separate.
- `Release Control` owns the general distribution of performer and composer
  agency. Shiroishi's unequal present/past relation is introduced here because
  it is audible as layering.
- `Sustain` owns the acoustical interior of drones and beating as such. Niblock
  is used here to study what many sustained strata do to one another.

## Failure modes to address

- Counting parts and calling the result density.
- Adding layers as a substitute for deciding what changes.
- Giving every strand the same register, attack, activity, and dynamic, then
  trying to repair the masking by making everything louder.
- Separating every strand so completely that the music becomes a demonstration
  rather than a field of consequential relations.
- Treating saturation as automatically climactic or clearing as automatically
  peaceful.
- Letting a captured or repeating layer remain after its formal job has ended.

## Exercises

### Notice: When Two Become One

Make or find three sustained strands. Change their tuning, register,
articulation, or common motion one variable at a time and note when they are
heard as three lines, one composite body, or an unstable intermediate state.

### Isolate: Mask Without Muting

Write three continuing strands. Make each one disappear perceptually and
return without stopping it or changing its dynamic. Use register, activity,
similarity, and surrounding attacks to alter its legibility.

### Complicate: Archive and Answer

Let a performer create several short layers over time, either with live capture
or with other players preserving each gesture. Require each new action to
reinterpret an earlier layer, and define a meaningful condition for clearing
the field.

## Working length

Approximately 5,800–7,000 words. The chapter needs room for the perceptual
mechanism, three contrasting practices, a usable decision map, failure modes,
and experiments, but should remain leaner than `Repeat` because it does not need
a historical taxonomy.

## Desired ending

Return to Shiroishi after the field clears. A layer is not the sound put down
earlier; it is what that sound has become in the current relation. End by
turning inward: once a strand is audible, its own attack, grain, pressure, and
decay may carry an argument before another layer arrives. That is the question
of color.
