# Chapter Brief: Sustain

## Central question

How can one sounding event become a field of activity rather than an inert
thing waiting to be replaced?

## Chapter promise

By the end of the chapter, the reader should be able to distinguish a sustained
pitch from the changing sound that carries it; hear beating, spectral balance,
resonance, renewal, and decay as compositional motion; use drones, pedals, and
reference tones for different jobs; shape entrances and releases as parts of
the sustained event; and write for the physical work by which performers keep
a sound alive.

## Central claim

To sustain is not simply to prevent a note from ending. It is to maintain a
relationship among energy, spectrum, tuning, body, room, and attention. A
nominal pitch can remain while its internal balance changes continuously.
Sustained form begins when the composer chooses which of those motions the
listener can enter, follow, and remember.

The chapter is organized mainly through changes in material and listener:

1. Material: spectrum, intensity, tuning, articulation, and resonance evolve.
2. Listener: attention moves from the fact of a sound to its internal activity.
3. Relationships: another pitch, body, or room makes beating and resonance
   audible.
4. Context: instruments and architecture determine what can continue and how.

## Opening and return

Open with La Monte Young's recollections of wind around a log cabin and the
continuous hum of power lines and transformers. These sounds were present
before composition, stable enough to enter and variable enough to keep
listening.

Return to the transformer at the end. A drone is not copied from the world by
holding one keyboard key. It is composed by deciding which internal relations
can remain active and when release will expose what the sound has changed.

## Primary encounters

### La Monte Young, sustained sounds and listening history

- Childhood memories as first-person testimony, not a single origin myth.
- A sustained environment contains changes of overtone, intensity, tuning,
  position, and weather.
- Later work turns long duration and tuning relations into spaces for
  inhabitation rather than merely extended notes.
- Acknowledge the many musical encounters beyond the childhood story,
  including Young's study with Pandit Pran Nath; do not present sustained sound
  as an isolated American invention.

### Giacinto Scelsi, `Quattro pezzi su una nota sola`

- Four movements, each centered on one pitch, for chamber orchestra.
- “One note” is a center rather than one unchanging frequency: octave,
  microtonal deviation, dynamics, articulation, register, and instrumental
  color animate it.
- Each central pitch supports a distinct behavior and form.
- The work shows why reducing pitch succession can intensify attention to the
  depth of sound.

### Alvin Lucier, `I Am Sitting in a Room`

- A spoken text is repeatedly sounded and returned through one room.
- Speech intelligibility recedes while resonant frequencies shared by voice and
  architecture grow prominent.
- The simple process reveals selection: the room reinforces some frequencies
  and lets others decay.
- Lucier's stated relation to his stutter keeps the process embodied and
  personal; do not describe it only as a neutral acoustics demonstration.

## Required theoretical ground

- Attack, bloom, continuation, renewal, release, decay, resonance, and
  aftersound as overlapping phases rather than a universal envelope.
- A musical pitch as a complex and changing spectrum, not a single frequency.
- Beating between close frequencies: audible amplitude fluctuation at the
  frequency difference in the simplest case.
- Difference or combination tones, carefully distinguished from beats and not
  promised in every situation.
- Instrument and room resonance as selective continuation.
- Sympathetic resonance and the return of energy through another body.
- Drone, pedal, and reference tone as different possible jobs; defer full
  harmonic function to `Make a Harmonic World`.
- Continuous versus renewed sustain: bow change, breath, tremolo, repeated
  attack, feedback, and resonance create different forms of continuity.
- Sustaining as performer labor: pressure, air, balance, fatigue, and
  correction remain in the sound.
- The formal roles of onset, internal threshold, withdrawal, and release.
- Loud sustained sound as physical pressure; intensity and exposure require
  care and consent.

## Boundaries

- `Attend` owns general attentional width. Sustain discusses the shift from
  hearing an entrance to hearing an interior.
- `Experience Time` owns duration and acclimation. Sustain asks what physically
  and musically changes during a held event.
- `Make a Harmonic World` owns chord function, modal fields, voice-leading, and
  tonal gravity. Pedals appear here as reference tones and acoustic relations.
- `Drift` owns independent clocks and breath-governed coordination. Breath here
  concerns renewal inside one continuing sound.
- `Layer` owns relations among many simultaneous strands. A second frequency
  enters here only to reveal beating or resonance within a composite sound.
- `Color` owns the general theory of timbre. Sustain develops the temporal and
  acoustical interior that Color deliberately deferred.
- `Place` owns rooms and sites as the main formal condition. Lucier's room is
  told here once because it reveals resonance; Place may make only a brief
  callback or use another work.
- `Release Control` owns Scelsi's improvisation and assistants. This chapter
  analyzes the credited work's audible evidence and does not tell the labor or
  authorship story.

## Failure modes to address

- Holding a note longer and assuming duration has created an interior.
- Adding undirected modulation whenever attention falters.
- Making every parameter drift at once, leaving no stable reference against
  which motion can be heard.
- Treating beating as a decorative effect rather than a tunable rate and form.
- Confusing a drone with a low pitch or a pedal with static harmony.
- Notating impossible continuity while erasing the breaths, bow changes, or
  exchanges that produce it.
- Equating sustained sound with calm, spirituality, or low volume.
- Ignoring hearing safety and a listener's inability to leave a loud field.

## Exercises

### Notice: Follow the Decay

Begin with one struck or plucked sound. Make no new event. Track three distinct
changes in its decay, then repeat the sound and enter only at one chosen
threshold.

### Isolate: Tune the Motion

Have two performers sustain a comfortable unison. Let one move slightly away
and return while the other remains stable. Shape a short form using only the
rate and clarity of beating, without treating “in tune” as the only goal.

### Complicate: One Sound, Three Forms of Continuity

Keep one pitch or interval present through three kinds of continuation:
unbroken energy, bodily renewal, and sympathetic or room resonance. Let the
transitions among these conditions carry the form.

## Working length

Approximately 5,800–7,000 words. The chapter needs enough acoustics to make
musical decisions exact, three substantial practices, a performer-centered
account of continuity, safety and failure modes, and experiments without
becoming a physics or production manual.

## Desired ending

Return to Young's remembered transformer. Its apparent sameness becomes a
field of small relations. End by letting its reference pitch raise the next
chapter's question: once a tone can remain, how can other tones gather around
it and create a harmonic world without needing a conventional destination?
