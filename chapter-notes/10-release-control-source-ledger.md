# Source Ledger: Release Control

## Julius Eastman, `Stay On It`

- Claim: The work dates from 1973, permits open instrumentation, and organizes
  repeated themes and cells, performer choice, optional improvisation and
  poetry, and visual or musical cues.
- Source: Wise Music Classical official work page and performance notes.
  https://www.wisemusicclassical.com/work/57872/Stay-On-It--Julius-Eastman/
- Confidence: HIGH for the current performance edition.
- Caveat: The edition is an editorial realization, not an untouched definitive
  manuscript.

- Claim: No complete original manuscript is known; early practice depended on
  sparse written material, rehearsal, performance, and oral transmission.
  Modern versions draw on recordings, witnesses, research, and editorial
  judgment.
- Sources: Matthew Mendez, “That Piece Does Not Exist without Julius,” in
  `Gay Guerrilla`; NPR/WRTI account of Wild Up's reconstruction; Mary Jane
  Leach's archive.
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/gay-guerrilla/that-piece-does-not-exist-without-julius-still-staying-on-stay-on-it/EDCEE9937AABE734351B1F414FFB2E57
  https://www.wrti.org/latest-classical-from-npr-music/latest-classical-from-npr-music/2022-05-18/wild-up-stay-on-it
  https://www.mjleach.com/EastmanScores.htm
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Caveat: Do not suggest that loss improved the work or that one modern edition
  recovers a final original version.

## Christian Wolff, `For 1, 2 or 3 People`

- Claim: The 1964 work consists of ten pieces from which a performance may
  select; instrumentation and number of players remain open. Pitch may be
  relatively unspecified while temporal and responsive relations are exact.
- Source: Christian Wolff first-person BOMB interview.
  https://bombmagazine.org/articles/1997/04/01/christian-wolff/
- Corroboration and score discussion: Dartmouth celebration programme;
  peer-reviewed `Circuit` article on cueing and performer constraint.
  https://eamusic.dartmouth.edu/~larry/miscellaneous_archival_documents/programs/Wolff_FINAL.pdf
  https://www.erudit.org/fr/revues/circuit/2020-v30-n2-circuit05461/1071119ar.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Use: Wolff says he imagined extreme allowable realizations and rejected or
  accepted the system accordingly. This supports testing rather than relying on
  vague trust.

## Giacinto Scelsi, recorded improvisation and transcription

- Claim: Many mature works began as recorded improvisations, often on the
  Ondiola, and were transcribed into conventional notation by hired assistants.
  Transcription introduced instrumental, aesthetic, and notational decisions.
- Sources: Marco Fusi's peer-reviewed archival study; Fondazione Isabella
  Scelsi archive description; Frances-Marie Uitti's first-person account.
  https://onlinepublishing.cini.it/index.php/arno/article/view/202
  https://www.scelsi.it/it/fondo-giacinto-scelsi/
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/F27DBB92D3516449BD0ACABBA0591AEC/S0040298200004484a.pdf/preserving_the_scelsi_improvisations.pdf
- Confidence: HIGH.

- Claim: Vieri Tosatti was a major collaborator/transcriber and later made
  strong authorship claims. Archival research complicates both Scelsi's
  self-mythology and a simple ghostwriter verdict.
- Sources: Fondazione Isabella Scelsi conference description based on primary
  archives; Foundation journal scholarship; Treccani biography.
  https://www.scelsi.it/en/musica-e-tecnologia-negli-anni-cinquanta--la-creativita-integrata-di-giacinto-scelsi-e-vieri-tosatti/
  https://www.scelsi.it/dati/pub/3/doc/44.pdf
  https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giacinto-francesco-maria-scelsi_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/
- Confidence: HIGH for collaboration and controversy; CONTESTED for assigning
  a simple percentage or final authorship verdict.

## John Cage, `Music of Changes`

- Claim: Cage composed the 1951 piano work using the `I Ching` and charts to
  select musical events. David Tudor premiered it. The resulting score fixes a
  demanding performance rather than granting the pianist broad choice.
- Sources: John Cage Complete Works database; James Pritchett's scholarship on
  Cage's chance procedures.
  https://www.data-johncage.org/pp/John-Cage-Work-Detail.cfm?work_ID=134
  https://musicofjohncage.com/writings/
- Confidence: HIGH for work, chance method, and fixed performance distinction.
- Cultural caveat: Do not reduce the `I Ching` to a neutral random-number
  device or imply that Cage's use exhausts its philosophical and cultural
  meaning.

## Brian Eno and Peter Schmidt, *Oblique Strategies*

- Claim: Eno and Schmidt jointly published *Oblique Strategies* in 1975 after
  discovering that they had independently developed collections of prompts for
  difficult working situations.
- Sources: Brian Eno's first-person account in Glenn O'Brien's 1978 interview;
  Geeta Dayal's Rhizome history, which also cites Eno's 1980 KPFA interview;
  the current official Eno Shop description.
  https://www.interviewmagazine.com/music/new-again-brian-eno
  https://old.rhizome.org/editorial/2009/oct/21/brian-eno-peter-schmidt-and-cybernetics/
  https://enoshop.co.uk/products/oblique-strategies
- Confidence: HIGH.
- Caveat: Eno and Schmidt's separate collections had antecedents and the deck
  changed across editions. Do not imply that every quoted card appeared in the
  first edition.

- Claim: Eno used the cards when work reached an impasse, drawing at random to
  interrupt linear effort and shift perception or approach. The cards selected
  a way of working rather than directly specifying a finished musical result.
- Sources: Eno's 1978 first-person interview and Dayal's account of the 1980
  interview, above.
- Confidence: HIGH.

- Claim and quotation check: Documented cards include “Use an old idea,”
  “Repetition is a form of change,” and “Humanize something free of error.”
- Sources: current official Eno Shop listing; Museu d'Art Contemporani de
  Barcelona's Ràdio Web MACBA publication; Dayal's Rhizome article.
  https://enoshop.co.uk/products/oblique-strategies
  https://img.macba.cat/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/18Interruptions_eng-1.pdf
  https://old.rhizome.org/editorial/2009/oct/21/brian-eno-peter-schmidt-and-cybernetics/
- Confidence: HIGH that these wordings have circulated as cards; MEDIUM for

- Copyright and editorial caveat: Quote only these three short cards (fifteen

## Section-specific interpretations

- A score distributes decisions rather than simply containing more or less
  control.
- Releasing a decision is meaningful only when another agent has sufficient
  information, skill, and authority to make it.
- The composer remains responsible for designing and testing the family of
  outcomes, even when no single result was directly selected.
- Work identity may persist in relations and social practice, but openness must
  remain capable of producing an outcome that does not belong.
- A randomly selected instruction can interrupt a composer's habitual frame
  without absolving that composer of hearing, choosing, or revising what
  follows.
