## Tōru Takemitsu wartime listening episode

- **Claims:** At fourteen, Takemitsu was assigned to wartime labor in the hills
  west of Tokyo; an officer clandestinely played `Parlez-moi d’amour` on a
  wind-up gramophone using a handmade bamboo needle; patriotic music dominated
  the boys’ official sound world; Takemitsu later described the song as an
  enormous shock and associated the experience with becoming a composer.
- **Primary/near-primary trail:** Takemitsu’s recollections as cited by Alex
  Ross and by the scholarly biographical literature referenced in the existing
  anecdote bank.
- **Independent checks:**
  - Alex Ross, “Toward Silence,” *The New Yorker*, January 29, 2007:
    https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2007/02/05/toward-silence
  - IRCAM, Tōru Takemitsu biography:
    https://brahms.ircam.fr/en/composer/toru-takemitsu/biography
- **Confidence:** Strong for age, military labor, clandestine playback,
  gramophone, bamboo needle, song title, and lasting importance.
- **Caveat:** Accounts disagree over whether the particular recording was sung
  by Lucienne Boyer or Josephine Baker. The Introduction names the song but not
  the singer. The episode is presented as a later recollection and catalyst,
  not as a complete causal origin for Takemitsu’s musical life.
- **Interpretive use:** A materially fragile event can acquire large structural
  force because of the field it enters. The text explicitly does not classify
  the chanson as ambient or minimalist.
